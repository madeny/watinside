Packing is the easy part. The nightmare starts three days later when you are standing in a new living room surrounded by 40 identical brown boxes, and the coffee maker could be in any of them.

A **moving inventory app** fixes this. You log what goes in each box, label the box, and then scan any box to see its contents, or search an item to find its box. No more "open everything and hope."

But which one is the **best moving inventory app** in 2026? We compared the popular options on price, speed, and how much prep they demand before moving day.

---

## What Makes a Good Moving Inventory App

Before you install anything, know what separates a real tool from a glorified notes app:

* **Fast capture.** You are packing a whole house. If logging a box takes two minutes, you will quit by box five. Photo-first entry with **AI tagging** is the only thing that keeps up.
* **Flexible labels.** You should not have to buy anything. Printable QR codes, reusable NFC tags, or a code you write with a marker — your choice, not the app's.
* **Offline and private.** Moving day has bad Wi-Fi. And your inventory is a list of everything you own — it should not live on a company's server.
* **Search, not just lists.** "Which box has the phone chargers?" should return an answer in one tap.

## The Best Moving Inventory Apps Compared

### 1. WatInside — Best Overall for Moves
**WatInside** is built for iOS and runs its image recognition on the iPhone's Neural Engine, so photos never leave your device. You snap a picture of what is going in the box, AI suggests the item names, and you attach a label.

* **Pros:** Free during early access. No accounts. QR **and** NFC **and** handwritten-label support. AI auto-tagging. Works offline. iCloud sync and family sharing.
* **Cons:** iOS only — no Android version yet.
* **Best for:** Anyone who wants to pack fast without buying stickers or trusting a cloud with their belongings.

**Why it wins the move:** the [handwritten label scanner](/handwritten-label-scanner/). Out of printer ink at 11pm? Write "K-04" on the box with a Sharpie, scan your own handwriting later, and it links to the digital box. Zero prep.

### 2. Sortly — Best for Businesses, Not Moves
**Sortly** is powerful but has pivoted toward business and team inventory. The free plan caps you at 100 items and one user, and CSV/PDF export plus QR printing are locked behind paid tiers that start around $49/month.

* **Pros:** Strong custom fields, good for teams tracking stock long-term.
* **Cons:** 100-item free limit is nothing for a house move. Subscription pricing. Cloud-based.
* **Best for:** Small businesses, not a one-time relocation.

### 3. ToteScan — Best If You Like Buying Stickers
**ToteScan** popularized bin scanning. The app is free, but it only works with their proprietary label packs, which run about $12–$16 a pack.

* **Pros:** Simple, durable pre-printed labels.
* **Cons:** Run out mid-move and you are waiting on shipping. Locked to their stickers. Heavy manual entry.
* **Best for:** People who plan weeks ahead and do not mind the label cost. (See our full [ToteScan alternative](/totescan-alternative/) breakdown.)

### 4. Everspruce — Solid, but Cross-Platform Compromises
**Everspruce** is a capable home inventory app with QR label printing and a free tier of about 50 entries. Being iOS, Android, and web at once, it does not lean on iPhone-specific features like on-device AI or NFC the way a native app can.

* **Pros:** Works on any platform, clean tag system.
* **Cons:** 50-entry free cap. No handwritten recognition. No NFC tap-to-view.
* **Best for:** Households split across Android and iPhone.

## The No-Printer Workflow (Why Movers Pick WatInside)

Here is the actual moving-day process with WatInside:

1.  **Create a box** in the app and photograph what goes in it. AI names the items.
2.  **Label it** — grab a printed QR code, tap an NFC tag, or just write a short code on the cardboard with a marker.
3.  **Tape it shut.** The physical box and the digital list are now linked.
4.  **At the new place,** scan any box to see inside, or search "winter coats" to find which box and room they are in — before you cut a single strip of tape.

No sticker packs. No subscription. No cloud account holding a map of your valuables.
